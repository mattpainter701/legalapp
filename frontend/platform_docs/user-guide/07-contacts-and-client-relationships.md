# Contacts & client relationships

[Contacts](/contacts) is the firm-wide relationship directory. A contact can represent a client, prospect, opposing party, attorney, court, vendor, expert, or another participant. Good contact data reduces duplicate work and keeps matter activity connected to the correct person or organization.

## Find before you create

Search by name, organization, email, or phone before adding a record. Check spelling variations and prior names. If a likely match exists, open it and verify the details rather than creating a duplicate.

When creating a contact, capture the display name, contact type, organization, preferred email and phone, address, and any operational notes your firm permits. Use neutral, factual language. Do not place legal strategy, health information, payment credentials, or privileged advice in a general contact note.

## Contact profile

The contact detail view has four working areas:

- **Profile** contains identity and communication details.
- **Matters** shows files connected to the contact.
- **Communications** shows logged exchanges associated with the contact.
- **Tasks** shows work involving the contact.

Review these relationships before changing identity fields. A renamed organization or corrected email may affect many active matters.

## Link contacts to matters

Open the relevant matter from [My Matters](/matters) and assign the contact with the correct role. Distinguish the client, billing contact, opposing party, counsel, witness, and other participants. A person may have different roles on different matters.

Do not infer that a contact has portal access merely because they are attached to a matter. Portal invitations and sharing are separate, deliberate actions.

## Communications and follow-up

Log meaningful contact activity through [Communications](/communications) or the related matter workflow. Create a [Task](/tasks) when a promise, callback, document request, or deadline needs an owner. Avoid using unstructured contact notes as a hidden task list.

## Data quality checks

Before relying on a contact record, confirm:

1. the identity matches the intended person or organization;
2. the email and phone are current;
3. matter relationships use the correct role;
4. duplicate records have been handled through the approved process; and
5. communication preferences and restrictions are respected.

If a record appears to belong to another tenant or unrelated client, stop work and notify an administrator rather than editing it.
