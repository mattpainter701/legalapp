# LegalApp brand-name research

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Decision stage:** Naming strategy and preliminary knockout screening  
**Current working name:** Clarity Legal  
**Recommendation:** Advance **MatterArc** to formal trademark clearance, with **FirmThread** and **Matterline** as the two backups.

> This is a strategic naming and preliminary screening report, not a legal opinion or a comprehensive trademark clearance search. Indexed-web and secondary trademark searches can miss pending, state, foreign, and common-law rights. Trademark counsel should clear the final candidates before public adoption.

## Executive decision

The best fit is **MatterArc**.

It gives the company a credible, attorney-native masterbrand without trapping the product in its launch wedge. “Matter” is the organizing unit of legal work; “Arc” expresses the full journey from first inquiry through task assignment, documents, research, billing, and closure. The name supports a clean promise:

> **Every matter, moving forward.**

It also gives the commercial story a memorable shape:

> **From first call to final invoice.**

The exact `matterarc.com` domain was publicly listed for sale for $499 in the search snapshot reviewed for this report. That is unusually favorable for a credible two-word `.com`, but the price and availability must be rechecked immediately before purchase.

The recommended decision set is:

1. **MatterArc** — best overall masterbrand.
2. **FirmThread** — best “one connected firm” alternative.
3. **Matterline** — best launch-wedge story, but its `.com` was listed at $4,999 and the name is slightly more telecommunications-oriented.

Do not continue with **Clarity Legal**. The issue is much broader than one abandoned registration: there is active legal software using Clarity Legal, several active law firms and legal-service businesses use the wording internationally, and a California law practice uses it in the United States. The name is also weakly distinctive and difficult to own in search.

## What is being named

The repository describes a multi-tenant, AI-assisted legal operations platform combining:

- caller intake and Zoom Phone workflows;
- task assignment, follow-up accountability, and partner/attorney handoff;
- matters, contacts, parties, documents, timelines, and communications;
- templates and reviewed document generation;
- time, billing, payments, trust accounting, and reporting;
- Microsoft, Google, Zoom, QuickBooks, Teams, and storage integrations;
- private firm knowledge plus source-aware public legal research.

The commercial launch is intentionally narrower: **Call Intake + Tasks + Zoom Phone**, with an upgrade path into the full platform. That creates a naming constraint: the name must sell responsiveness and operational control now without sounding like only a receptionist, phone, CRM, or AI product.

### Core audiences

| Audience | What the name needs to signal |
|---|---|
| Receptionist / intake coordinator | Calls are captured, details stay organized, handoffs do not disappear |
| Attorney / assignee | The right facts and next action are visible without extra admin |
| Partner / practice owner | Leads are answered, accountability is measurable, the firm is under control |
| Firm administrator / operations lead | One workflow across intake, tasks, matters, documents, and billing |
| IT / security reviewer | Serious, durable, integrated software—not an experimental AI toy |

## Market and buyer evidence

The naming brief follows the buying reality rather than generic “legal innovation” language:

- Clio’s client-intake research found that only 40% of contacted firms answered the phone and 48% remained unreachable by phone after follow-up opportunities. This validates the launch wedge: responsiveness and reliable handoff are commercially meaningful problems.
- The 2025 Legal Trends Report says firms prioritize saving time, managing caseloads, ensuring quality, and improving client experience when buying technology.
- Clio’s solo/small-firm research reported that digital intake tools correlate with higher leads, conversion, and revenue, while broad AI adoption among solos and small firms remained cautious.
- The ABA reported that 73% of firms use cloud-based legal tools, while security policies and adoption maturity remain uneven. Trust, workflow fit, and security therefore matter more than an “AI-first” name.
- Current category leaders use memorable, compact masterbrands—Clio, MyCase, Smokeball, Filevine, Actionstep, Lawmatics—rather than long descriptive corporate names.

### Naming implications

1. **Do not put “AI” in the masterbrand.** AI is a capability and proof point, not the durable category.
2. **Do not make “Legal” part of the protectable core.** It is descriptive, crowded, and can make a software company look like a law firm.
3. **Avoid the “clarity/clear” family.** It is saturated across firms, legal services, compliance, and software.
4. **Avoid narrow phone or intake naming.** It would fight the planned expansion into firm-wide operations.
5. **Prefer movement, continuity, and control.** Those benefits unite the launch product and full platform.
6. **Use a clear descriptor under the mark.** Example: “Legal operations for modern firms.” This preserves comprehension while the masterbrand remains ownable.

## Candidate briefs

### 1. MatterArc — recommended

**Why it works**

- “Matter” is native to attorneys, paralegals, administrators, billing, and documents.
- “Arc” turns the platform into a lifecycle rather than a pile of modules.
- It supports the launch wedge: the arc begins at the first call.
- It supports the future platform: the arc continues through work, documents, research, billing, and resolution.
- It is serious without sounding institutional or old-fashioned.
- Exact-name web searches did not surface an active legal or software brand during this screening.
- Indexed secondary trademark searches did not surface an exact `MATTERARC` result during this screening.
- `matterarc.com` was publicly listed for sale at $499 in the reviewed snapshot.

**Risks**

- “Matter” is heavily used in legal technology, so the full combined mark—not “Matter” alone—must carry distinctiveness.
- “Arc” is common in technology names. Similar marks, phonetic equivalents, state registrations, and common-law uses still require counsel review.
- The lifecycle meaning needs to be taught once in launch messaging; after that, it becomes an asset.

**Recommended pronunciation:** `MAT-er ark`  
**Elevator line:** MatterArc keeps every client inquiry, assignment, document, deadline, and dollar on one connected path.  
**Primary tagline:** Every matter, moving forward.  
**Campaign line:** From first call to final invoice.

### 2. FirmThread

**Why it works**

- Makes continuity and handoff the hero.
- Encompasses receptionists, staff, attorneys, and partners better than an attorney-only name.
- Extends naturally across calls, tasks, conversations, matter history, files, and reporting.
- Exact-name searches were relatively clean during this review.

**Risks**

- “Thread” is associated with email, messaging, and the smart-home networking standard, which adds search noise.
- The benefit is less explicitly legal than MatterArc and needs a descriptor.
- Exact `.com` registration status was not conclusively established in this research environment.

**Taglines**

- One thread through every matter.
- Keep the whole firm in the loop.
- From first contact to finished work, connected.

### 3. Matterline

**Why it works**

- The strongest semantic bridge between the initial phone-intake product and a full matter platform.
- “Line” can mean phone line, throughline, line of responsibility, and direct path.
- Simple to pronounce and professional in a sales conversation.

**Risks**

- Can initially sound like a phone service unless the descriptor makes the platform breadth clear.
- `matterline.com` was listed for sale at $4,999 in the reviewed snapshot.
- A dissolved UK company previously used `MATTERLINE LTD`; it appeared unrelated, but counsel must still check common-law and international history.

**Taglines**

- The direct line from inquiry to outcome.
- Every call connected to the work that follows.
- Keep every matter on the line.

### 4. MatterNorth

**Why it works:** Calm, directional, credible, and broad enough for a platform. Exact-name web and indexed trademark searches were relatively clean.  
**Why it is not top three:** It describes guidance more than execution, and the metaphor is less closely tied to the product’s accountability and handoff strengths.

### 5. CounselThread

**Why it works:** Attorney-centered and strongly connected to knowledge continuity.  
**Why it is not top three:** “Counsel” can imply in-house counsel, a law firm, or legal advice, and it underrepresents receptionists and operational staff who are central to the launch product.

### 6. MatterSpring

**Why it works:** Suggests momentum, renewal, and growth.  
**Why it is not top three:** Slightly less serious and precise; “spring” can feel like a campaign or consultancy rather than core infrastructure.

### 7–8. RecordRelay and DocketRelay

Both make handoff and accountability easy to understand. RecordRelay is broad but can sound like health-record or data-transfer software. DocketRelay sounds legal but biases toward litigation and courts, excluding estate planning, transactional, intake-only, and general practice customers.

## Recommended brand system

Use a compact masterbrand and keep descriptive language outside the mark:

### Corporate / masterbrand

**MatterArc**  
Legal operations for modern firms.

### Product architecture

- MatterArc Intake
- MatterArc Tasks
- MatterArc Matters
- MatterArc Documents
- MatterArc Research
- MatterArc Billing
- MatterArc Integrations

This architecture is clearer and more defensible than inventing a separate brand for every module. The launch offer can be sold as **MatterArc Intake**, while the full suite remains **MatterArc**.

### Messaging hierarchy

1. **Brand promise:** Every matter, moving forward.
2. **Launch proof:** Capture every call. Assign every follow-up. See what happened next.
3. **Platform proof:** From first call to final invoice, one connected operating system.
4. **AI proof:** Source-aware assistance, human review, and visible provenance where judgment matters.
5. **Trust proof:** Tenant isolation, role-based access, audit trails, and controlled integrations.

## Verbal and visual direction

### Voice

- Calm, exact, plain-spoken, and operational.
- Lead with outcomes and proof, not “revolutionary,” “disruptive,” or “magical.”
- Say “assists,” “routes,” “tracks,” “shows,” and “requires review.”
- Avoid anthropomorphizing the platform as an “AI lawyer.”

### Identity

- Use an arc as a path with visible checkpoints, not a generic swoosh.
- Favor deep navy or ink, warm paper, a restrained copper/orange progress accent, and a muted teal verification accent.
- Combine a confident editorial serif for headlines with a highly legible humanist sans for product UI.
- Avoid gavels, courthouse columns, scales of justice, robot heads, sparkles, and neon AI gradients.

## Attorney/end-user validation plan

Do not ask, “Which name do you like?” Run a blind comprehension and trust test with 15–20 people:

- 6–8 practicing attorneys across solo, small, and mid-size firms;
- 3–4 partners or firm administrators with buying authority;
- 4–5 receptionists, intake coordinators, paralegals, or legal assistants;
- 2–3 legal IT/security or operations reviewers.

Test the top three in randomized order without logos.

### Questions

1. What do you think this product does?
2. Who do you think it is for?
3. How would you pronounce it?
4. After hearing it once, how would you spell it?
5. How credible would it feel in a partner meeting? (1–7)
6. How comfortable would you be seeing it handle client information? (1–7)
7. Does it sound like a law firm, a consumer service, or software?
8. Which name best fits “from first client call through the life of a matter”?
9. What negative association, if any, does the name create?
10. Which two would you be willing to see demonstrated?

### Pass thresholds

- At least 80% pronounce it consistently.
- At least 70% spell it correctly after hearing it once.
- Median credibility and data-trust scores of 5 or higher out of 7.
- Fewer than 20% mistake it for a law firm or consumer legal service.
- No recurring negative association among attorneys or staff.
- The winner appears in at least 60% of respondents’ top two.

## Clearance and acquisition sequence

1. **Freeze three candidates:** MatterArc, FirmThread, Matterline.
2. **Commission counsel-led knockout searches:** exact marks, phonetic equivalents, spacing/plurals, related “Matter + Arc” combinations, and translations where commercially relevant.
3. **Run comprehensive clearance:** USPTO federal records, state marks and entity records, common-law web/news/database use, WIPO/Madrid and priority foreign markets, app stores, software directories, social handles, and domain history.
4. **Map goods/services with counsel:** likely Class 42 for SaaS; evaluate Class 9 for downloadable/on-prem agent software and any other classes supported by actual launch use. Do not claim legal services merely because customers are lawyers.
5. **Secure domains and handles:** after knockout confidence, acquire the exact `.com` where practical plus defensive variants. Domain ownership is not trademark clearance.
6. **File an intent-to-use application:** before broad public reveal if the company has not yet used the mark in interstate commerce.
7. **Run the end-user test:** legal availability is mandatory, but audience comprehension selects among legally viable names.
8. **Approve the brand system:** final wordmark, descriptor, module naming, pronunciation, and usage rules.
9. **Plan the code/content migration:** inventory `Clarity Legal`, `claritylegal`, `clarity-*`, domains, OAuth applications, email templates, cloud folder names, package names, telemetry keys, documentation, and customer-visible certificates before replacement.

## Immediate next actions

- Ask trademark counsel to clear **MatterArc**, **FirmThread**, and **Matterline** in that order.
- Recheck the `matterarc.com` listing and, if counsel’s initial knockout is favorable, decide quickly whether the $499 acquisition is acceptable.
- Reserve practical fallback domains such as `getmatterarc.com`, `usematterarc.com`, and `matterarclegal.com` only after confirming current availability and ownership history.
- Run the blind attorney/staff test using the script above.
- Keep **Clarity Legal** in code until the final mark and domain are secured; premature technical renaming creates rework and can expose the selected name before filing.
