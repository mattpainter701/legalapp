# Ohio and North Dakota Local Authority Sources

Research date: 2026-07-31.  This is an acquisition assessment, not legal advice.

## Decision summary

Use official state and court sites as the citation/canonical URL layer.  Automate only where the site exposes a documented API, bulk/download files, or an express-permission review has cleared the retrieval method.  Do **not** crawl Ohio Laws: its live `robots.txt` is `User-agent: * Disallow: /`.  Treat commercial municipal-code hosts and Secretary-of-State transactional portals as lookup-only until their terms are cleared.

The initial production corpus should be: ND Century Code and ND Administrative Code chapter PDFs; ND Supreme Court opinions, rules, and self-help probate/mediation materials; Ohio Supreme Court statewide rules/forms/opinions and the CourtListener public mirror for historical Ohio opinions; Ohio and ND AG/ethics/professional-conduct opinions; and selected county court sources through a reviewed county manifest.  Store every source URL, retrieval timestamp, content hash, effective date, jurisdiction, publisher, authority level, and `acquisition_basis` (`api`, `bulk_download`, `permitted_fetch`, `manual_upload`, or `licensed`).

## Retrieval and storage rules

1. **Authority and acquisition are different fields.** `canonical_publisher=Ohio Laws` can coexist with `acquisition_publisher=manual upload` or `CourtListener`; never label a mirror as official.
2. **No blind domain crawler.** Use a reviewed manifest: immutable `source_id`, URL, publisher, document type, jurisdiction, check cadence, robots/terms status, and parser.  A new county or local code is a new review item.
3. **Keep documents legally time-aware.** Version by effective date and retrieval time.  Do not overwrite.  Link amendment/session-law/bill relationships where supplied.
4. **Separate legal text from workflow data.** Forms, filing fees, appointments, mediation rosters and property portals get their own document/workflow types and provenance, not an implied rule of law.
5. **Embeddings are derivative search aids.** Search snippets must return canonical URL, source publisher, date/effective date, authority rank, and exact text span.  Re-embed on hash change; retain prior embeddings by document version.
6. **Avoid sensitive/transactional data.** Do not bulk acquire court records, UCC debtor data, property-document images, or personal-information-heavy portals without a documented legal basis, minimization policy, retention schedule, and access controls.

## Recommended ingestion order

1. ND Century Code + ND Administrative Code, chapter-by-chapter PDF manifests; ND AG and Ethics opinions.
2. Ohio Supreme Court statewide rules, standard probate forms, mediation rules/forms, BPC/OEC opinions, plus latest official appellate opinion PDFs.
3. ND Supreme Court rules/forms/probate/mediation guides and opinions after a court-permission/robots decision.
4. CourtListener OH/ND historical opinions as an open mirror, with official-citation reconciliation and a coverage gap report.
5. Showcase-county manifests: each selected Ohio probate/mediation court, then named ND district/local courts.  Add local rules, standing orders, forms, fees, e-filing and mediation referral workflows.
6. Municipal code and property/business/UCC data only after license, written permission, or per-government open-data approval.

## Open alternatives and monitoring aids

- [Open States bulk data](https://open.pluralpolicy.com/data/) is useful for Ohio/ND bill and legislator discovery, but it is not the official text source and should not substitute for an enacted official PDF/code citation.
- [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/help/coverage/opinions/) is a strong open case-law mirror/API.  Use it for discovery, bulk history and citation graph work; mark its source class `open_mirror` and maintain a court-URL reconciliation process.
- Public Git repositories may be used only when their license, update provenance, and completeness are reviewed.  None located in this pass should be treated as a production replacement for the Ohio or ND official code.

These results are operational evidence, not a legal opinion on authorization; terms of use and a contact/permission record remain required before enabling each connector.
